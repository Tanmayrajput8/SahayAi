# Clinical Protocol: Acute Coronary Syndrome & Cardiac Arrest (PROTO-CARD-01)
Category: CARDIAC
Source: National Emergency Clinical Guidelines & SahayAI Emergency Protocol Handbook 2026

## Clinical Presentation & Symptoms
Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS), acute myocardial infarction (heart attack), and cardiac arrest are high-lethal cardiovascular emergencies. Cardinal presentation includes:
- Severe substernal or retrosternal chest pain (described as crushing, pressure, heavy weight, squeezing)
- Radiation of pain to left shoulder, arm, neck, jaw, or epigastrium
- Associated diaphoresis (profuse cold sweating), dizziness, sudden collapse, nausea, or vomiting
- Severe shortness of breath (dyspnea) and cyanosis
- Sudden loss of consciousness or pulselessness

## Required Ambulance Tier: ALS (Advanced Life Support)
Cardiac emergencies MANDATE an Advanced Life Support (ALS) ambulance. Basic Life Support (BLS) is contraindicated unless ALS is completely depleted and urgent paramedic CPR escort is dispatched.

## Mandatory Vehicle Equipment
1. Multi-parameter Monitor with 12-lead ECG capability
2. Automated External Defibrillator (AED) / Biphasic Manual Defibrillator
3. High-flow Supplemental Oxygen delivery system (non-rebreather mask)
4. Advanced Airway Management kit (Endotracheal tubes, supraglottic airways)
5. Intravenous (IV) access equipment, normal saline, aspirin 325mg, sublingual nitroglycerin

## Hospital Capabilities Required
The patient must be routed strictly to an active hospital with 24/7 Cardiac Care Unit (CCU), Catheterization Laboratory (Cath Lab for primary PCI), and emergency cardiologist on call.
